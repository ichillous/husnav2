One system, every screen.

Rules every screen follows

Decisions made once so that no page has to make them again.

Saving a page of fields

The moment anything on the page changes, a bar appears at the bottom of the window and stays there while you scroll. It counts the changes, says where they are, and holds Discard and Save. The top of the page shows the same state in words.

2 unsaved changes in Classes and Tuition. Nothing changes for families until you save.

Used on Program setup, Public profile and Create event. Leaving with changes unsaved asks first: Leave without saving?

Forms in steps

Back and Continue ride at the bottom of the window. If Continue is not ready, the bar says what is missing. Each step saves when you leave it.

One control per settings row

The label and its explanation sit on the left, a single control on the right, always in the same column. A value that only matters when the switch is on goes in its own row underneath.

Checking what was typed

A field is checked when you leave it, not while you type. The message sits under the field and says what to enter. Pressing Continue with errors moves to the first one. Fields are required unless the label says optional.

Enter the whole address, like name@example.com.
For urgent school alerts only.

The message names the fix, never the fault: “Enter a date as month, day, year”, not “Invalid date”.

Actions that cannot be undone

Red outline, never a red fill. If it can be reversed, do it and offer Undo. If it cannot, ask on the spot, say exactly what will happen, and for the largest ones ask the person to type the name.

How Husna talks

Plain, warm and specific. It says what happened and what happens next, in that order. It never blames, never shouts, and never leaves a parent wondering whether they were charged.

On a phone

The same pages, rearranged. The header keeps the logo, one or two actions and a Menu button; the menu is its own full screen. Numbers sit two to a row, forms go to one column, wide tables scroll inside their own box, and save and step bars stay at the bottom of the screen.

Usable by everyone

Built in from the first screen, not checked at the end.

  • Every control is a real button, link or input, with a visible label or a spoken one
  • Everything works from the keyboard, and the focus ring is always visible
  • Text meets 4.5 to 1 against what is behind it; status is a word, not only a color
  • Errors are announced, attached to their field, and say how to fix them
  • Motion is limited to switches and is turned off for people who ask for less

On a TV

One fixed frame, 1920 by 1080, read from the back row. The smallest type is 22 pixels and the clock is 168. Nothing can be pressed, so nothing looks pressable: no buttons, no links, no hover. The next prayer is the one sage block on the screen. The only things that move are the clock, one countdown and the change of slide, and all of it stops during the prayer.

The frames are row H on the canvas, from the pairing code to the quiet screen. They are set up on TV displays, and the same frame is the preview there.
